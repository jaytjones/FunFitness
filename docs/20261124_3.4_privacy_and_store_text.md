## 1. What Friends stores, and who can see it

| Data | Where | Who can read it | Developer access |
|---|---|---|---|
| Friends profile: display name, emoji, style, sharing switches, hashed Sign in with Apple ID | Person's private iCloud (`Friends` zone), shared with each friend | The person and their friends | **No** |
| Feed items (badge / challenge / streak), high fives, duels, weekly Effort Points | Same zone | The person and their friends | **No** |
| Open invites, pending accepts, blocked people | Person's private iCloud (`FriendsLocal`, not shared) | Only the person | **No** |
| Invite lookup: code, dates, the inviter's or invitee's **CloudKit user record name** (app-scoped iCloud ID), share link | CloudKit **public** database, deleted when used or after 7 days | Anyone holding the code; the link only opens for people added to the share | **Yes** (CloudKit Console) |
| Sign in with Apple user ID | Keychain on the device; only its SHA-256 leaves the device (in the profile above) | — | **No** |
| Workouts, routes, health data | Unchanged: device, Apple Health, the person's private iCloud | The person | **No** |

Apple's definition: data is *collected* when it's sent off the device in a way the developer (or a third
party) can access beyond real-time servicing. Only the invite lookup records meet that.

## 2. App Privacy (App Store Connect), at Friends launch

Add one data type; leave the rest as they are today.

| Data type | Collected | Linked to the user | Used for tracking | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Identifiers › User ID** | Yes | Yes | No | App Functionality |

- Rationale: the app-scoped CloudKit user record name sits briefly in the public database so two people
  can add each other to their shares. It's an identifier, so "linked"; it's never used for tracking,
  advertising or analytics.
- **Not** added: name, user content, fitness, contacts. Display names, unlocks and Effort Points go only to the
  person's own iCloud and their friends; the developer can't read them.
- Review interpretations vary; if App Review asks, describe it as above. Over-declaring "Other User Content"
  (not linked) is a safe alternative if Jay prefers.

## 3. Privacy policy: Friends section (add to FunFitness's privacy policy)

> **Friends (optional)**
>
> Friends lets you add people you invite, trade high fives and run weekly Effort Points duels. It's off until
> you sign in with Apple. You don't need it to use FunFitness.
>
> **What friends can see.** Your display name, emoji and style, plus only what you turn on in What Friends
> See: badges and challenges you unlock, streak milestones, and your weekly Effort Points. Your workouts,
> routes and health data are never shared.
>
> **Where it's kept.** Your Friends profile and anything you share are stored in your own iCloud account and
> shared through iCloud only with the friends you add. We (the developer) can't read them. When you send or
> accept an invite, a short-lived record with the invite code, an app-specific iCloud identifier and a link to
> your shared data is kept in FunFitness's iCloud database so your friend's app can find you; it's deleted once
> the invite is used or after 7 days, and the link only works for the friend you add.
>
> **Sign in with Apple.** We don't ask Apple for your name or email. FunFitness keeps your Apple sign-in
> identifier on your device and stores only a scrambled version of it with your Friends profile.
>
> **Your choices.** Turn any sharing switch off at any time (it also hides what you shared before). Remove or
> block a friend from their menu. Delete your Friends profile in the Friends tab to remove it and everything you
> shared; you disappear from friends' lists. Stopping "Sign in with Apple" for FunFitness in Settings does the
> same. Your workouts stay on your devices.
>
> **Limitations.** Friends works only on Apple devices signed in to iCloud. Duel totals come from each
> person's own app and aren't verified. Updates need an internet connection and can take a moment to arrive.

## 5. App Review notes (launch submission)

> Friends is optional and off by default. To review it you need two devices or simulators signed in to two
> different Apple IDs with iCloud:
> 1. On device A: Friends tab › Sign in with Apple › create a profile › Invite a Friend (note the 6-character
>    code).
> 2. On device B: Friends tab › sign in › create a profile › enter the code › Add.
> 3. Reopen the Friends tab on device A; each now lists the other. Turn on sharing switches in What Friends See
>    to see the feed, high fives, the leaderboard and duels.
>
> Account deletion: Friends tab › Delete Friends Profile (removes the profile and everything shared).
> Safety: friends are invite-only (no search, no strangers, no public content); each friend's menu has Remove
> and Block; blocked people can't send new invites. There is no free-form messaging; shared content is a display
> name, an emoji and app-generated items. Sign in with Apple is the only sign-in option.

## 6. Internal TestFlight: What to Test (first Friends build)

> Internal build (internal testers only). New: **Friends tab** (Pals / Squad / Flock depending on style).
